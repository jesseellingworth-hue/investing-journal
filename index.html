Klarna: AMEX for the Intelligence Era

October 24, 2026

Thesis

Klarna is a next-generation American Express in the making: a closed-loop network that is part bank, part payment network, part card issuer. The closest comp is Amex — network plus issuer plus lender — plus a deposit franchise. Licensed as a bank in Sweden since 2017; in the US it lends through partner banks.

The upside, on 2032 exit multiples on gross profit: at 12x transaction margin — about Affirm's multiple — $100B of equity value (about $256 a share) needs 2032 transaction margin of about $8.3B. From 2027's estimated $2.2B, that is about 31% a year for five years. At a Robinhood-like 18x, the same $100B needs only about $5.6B, about 21% a year. The card-led path reaches $8.7B by 2031 — the bull case is card delivery at a lender's multiple, not multiple expansion.

The network effect is the thesis. 120 million consumers, 1.2 million merchants (Exhibit 1). Every transaction improves underwriting and makes the network more valuable to the next merchant. Klarna no longer wins merchants one by one. Stripe, Nexi, JPMorgan Payments, and Fiserv's Clover now switch Klarna on by default, so their merchants get it automatically — merchant count rose from 966,000 to 1.2 million in two quarters. Walmart adds the same through OnePay.

The card converts that network into economics. Card users grew from 1.3 million (Q2 2025) to 6.5 million (Q2 2026), and a card user generates about $130 of revenue versus about $28 for the average consumer — nearly 5x (Exhibits 3, 6). Deposits cover 116% of receivables.

Profitability is two quarters old, not a track record. Operating income was negative through 2024–2025 except Q3 2024, then +$17M and +$27M in Q1 and Q2 2026. Transaction margin grew 42% year over year in Q2 against revenue up 27% — the gap is operating leverage. (FY2024's profit was an asset sale.)

Valuation is the other half. At $12.81, Klarna is worth about $4.8B — about 0.9x forward sales and 2.2x forward transaction margin (2027 estimates: revenue about $5.2B, transaction margin about $2.2B, up 32%). Affirm trades at about 4.4x forward sales and 8.9x forward gross profit; Dave at 5.3x and 7.4x; Chime at 3.6x and 4.0x (Exhibits 2–5).

What has to go right: provisions at or below 0.50% of GMV, US lending economics intact, no severe credit cycle, 4–7x card adoption, and contained dilution. The kill criteria are in the appendix; any two together force a re-underwrite.

Management: Sebastian Siemiatkowski

Klarna is still founder-led. Sebastian Siemiatkowski co-founded it in Stockholm in 2005 at 23 and has been CEO for 21 years — through profitable first 14 years, a Swedish banking licence in 2017, and the $45.6 billion to $6.7 billion collapse of 2021–22. He holds about 7.5%, sold nothing at the IPO, and bought about $10 million more at $14.37 on August 26, 2026, eight days after the guidance-cut crash.

His decade-old vision is a digital financial assistant: you wake up, it has analysed your mortgage while you slept, found you are overpaying by $30 a month, already negotiated the switch, paperwork done — all you do is say yes. He likens the timing to self-driving cars: you do not see them, then suddenly you are in a Waymo. The card, charter, savings, memberships, and Invest are all building toward that end-state, which is still mostly promise. He frames Klarna the way Amex framed itself — not a bank in consumers' minds, but a lifestyle brand with a card, referenced nine times in 51 minutes at Bernstein in May 2026 — with a wedge of fashion and beauty, $100–200 purchases, then big-ticket financing, then everyday spend, and a stated ambition to become one of the major retail banks in the world.

Underwriting $250 a share

$250 is not a multiple story. It is what happens if the operating plan lands. What can go right, in order: the card keeps compounding — 6.5 million users today, tens of millions by 2031, each worth roughly $130 of revenue against $28 for the average consumer — and that alone carries revenue per user from the mid-$30s toward $50 and beyond. Provisions stay at or below 0.50% of GMV while Fair Financing grows, so the extra volume drops to transaction margin instead of being eaten by credit costs. Distribution stays default-on — Stripe, Nexi, JPMorgan Payments, Clover — so merchants keep arriving without Klarna having to win them one by one. Deposits keep funding the book, memberships keep compounding from 2 million, and the US charter lands, so Klarna lends directly instead of through a partner bank.

Then the second leg: the assistant. If Seb's digital financial assistant actually ships — an agent that renegotiates your mortgage while you sleep, moves your deposits, switches your bills — Klarna stops being a checkout button and becomes the primary account. That is what earns a broker-like multiple on a lender's gross profit, rather than a bank multiple. None of this needs a heroic re-rate on today's numbers. It needs the card, the credit book, and the assistant to all land, in that order, over five years. If any two stall, $250 is off the table — see the kill criteria below.

What would change the thesis

Provisions back above 0.65% of GMV. Fair Financing delinquencies rising while growth slows — that combination means underwriting, not mix. Deposit outflows in two consecutive quarters. Card users below 15M by end-2027. Charter denied plus WebBank repricing. Any one alone does not kill it; two together force a full re-underwrite.
